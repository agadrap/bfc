  <h2 class="intro-heading">A Real-Life Siegfried</h2>
  <div class="prose">
    <p class="drop-cap"><em>Django Unchained</em> is my favourite out of Tarantino's entire repertoire. I absolutely adore it — with a cast including Jamie Foxx, Christoph Waltz, Leonardo DiCaprio, Samuel L. Jackson, and Kerry Washington, who wouldn't? But even the greatest actors won't make up for a poor story. A good script tackling a socially important issue is what makes <em>Django Unchained</em> the rare film that has it all: the acting, the story, the artistic vision, the humour, the music, and, finally, the hero.</p>
  </div>
</div>

  <div class="film-section-header">

  <div class="prose">
    <p class="drop-cap">Have you ever done something wrong that followed you around for years, like a piece of toilet paper stuck to your shoe? Well, America did — and it's called slavery. <em>Django Unchained</em> follows the story of a slave, Django (Jamie Foxx), in the 1850s, who was forced to part with his wife, Broomhilda (Kerry Washington), and is now on a quest to find her. Luckily for him, he happens to hold information useful to a bounty hunter, Dr. King Schultz (Christoph Waltz), who tracks him down and proposes a partnership.</p>
    <p>Meeting the doctor becomes a life-changing event for Django. Through it, he discovers strengths he didn't know he had — dignity, resilience, bravery, intelligence.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="pull-quote">
    <p class="pull-quote-text">Qualities of a real-life Siegfried.</p>
    <span class="pull-quote-accent"></span>
  </div>

  <div class="prose">
    <p>The film is full of bloody effects that recall a teenager's storytelling, aiming to impress his friends — slightly exaggerated, to say the least. A characteristic that has become Tarantino's hallmark by now. First and foremost, though, <em>Django Unchained</em> is enjoyable to watch, and it makes us want to believe that there is, still, justice in the world.</p>
  </div>
</div>
